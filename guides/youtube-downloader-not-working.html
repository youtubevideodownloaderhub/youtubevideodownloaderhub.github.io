YouTube Downloader Not Working? Fixes for the Most Common Errors

Why YouTube downloads fail and how to fix each error, from bad links to blocked videos and full storage.

Few things are more annoying than a download that fails at 90 percent, or a tool that insists a perfectly good video does not exist. Most download failures have a small number of causes, and each one has a straightforward check. This guide works through the common errors in order, from the link itself to your device and your connection, so you can find the problem quickly instead of trying random fixes.

Check the link first

Copy the link again directly from the video's Share button rather than typing it by hand. A single wrong character in the eleven-character video ID points to a different video or to nothing at all. Make sure the link is a full YouTube URL and not a link to a channel, a playlist, or a search page. If the link came from a message or an email, open it in a browser first to confirm the video still exists and still plays, because deleted and private videos fail in every tool.

The video itself may be the problem

Age-restricted videos require a signed-in adult account, private videos require the uploader's permission, and region-locked videos are unavailable in your country. Live streams cannot be downloaded while they are live, and very long videos sometimes fail on tools with size limits. If the video plays on YouTube but a tool cannot fetch it, the video may be using a format the tool does not support. In that case the official Premium download, where available, is the best fallback.

Connection and storage problems

A download that stalls usually means the connection dropped or the device ran out of space. Check your free storage before a large download: a two-hour video at 1080p needs several gigabytes. Switch to Wi-Fi, keep the phone or laptop awake, and close apps that are streaming or updating in the background. If you are on a VPN, try turning it off, because some VPN exit points are blocked or throttled by video services.

When the tool itself is broken

Third-party downloaders break often because YouTube changes how videos are delivered. If a tool worked last week and fails today, it may need an update from its developer, and waiting a day or two sometimes helps. Clear the tool's cache or try a different browser if the page itself misbehaves. Never pay a site that promises to fix a broken download, and never install a program it offers as a repair. If the official download feature also fails, the problem is usually with the video or your account, not the tool.

When to stop troubleshooting

There is a point where further troubleshooting wastes more time than the video is worth, and recognising it is a skill. If you have checked the link, confirmed the video plays on YouTube, tried the official download, checked your storage and connection, and tried a different browser or device, and it still fails, the video is almost certainly restricted by the uploader or a rights holder. No amount of retrying, reinstalling, or trying random tools will change that, and the tools that claim they can are usually scams. At that point the sensible move is to stream the video, add it to Watch Later or a playlist, or look for an officially licensed copy elsewhere. If the video is your own and it still will not download, contact YouTube support through YouTube Studio, because the problem may be with your account. Knowing when to stop is not giving up; it is spending your time on things that can actually be fixed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the downloader say the video is unavailable?

The video may be private, deleted, age-restricted, or region-locked. Check that it still plays on YouTube.

Why does my download stop halfway?

Interrupted connections, full storage, or a browser going to sleep are the usual causes. Try Wi-Fi and keep the device awake.

Does YouTube block downloader tools?

YouTube changes its systems regularly, which can break third-party tools. The official Premium download feature is the most reliable option.
