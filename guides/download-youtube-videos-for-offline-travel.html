Downloading YouTube Videos for Travel: Flights, Trains, and Offline Trips

Prepare YouTube videos for a trip: what to download, how much space you need, and how to keep them working offline.

A long flight or train ride is the best reason to download YouTube videos, and also the worst time to discover that your downloads do not work. A little preparation the night before makes the difference between a relaxing journey and hours of staring at an error message. This guide covers what to download, how to prepare your device, and the checks to run before you leave home.

Choose what to download

Pick videos you will actually watch, not everything that looks interesting. A mix works best: a few long videos for the main stretch, some short ones for boarding and waiting, and something light for when you are tired. For children, download their favourites plus one new thing. Remember that downloads expire after 30 days without an internet connection, so download the night before rather than a month ahead, and check that each video finished downloading before you leave.

Prepare your device

Charge your phone or tablet fully and bring a power bank, because video playback drains the battery. Download on Wi-Fi at home, where the connection is fast and free, and set the quality to 480p or 720p to fit more videos in. Test one download in airplane mode before you leave: turn on airplane mode, open the video, and confirm it plays. If it does not, you still have time to fix it at home, which you will not have at 35,000 feet.

Space and quality for a trip

A ten-hour flight needs roughly 10 hours of video, which at 480p is about 5 GB and at 1080p is about 20 GB. Most people overestimate how much they will watch and underestimate how much space they have, so 480p is the sensible choice for a phone screen on a plane. Bring headphones, because the built-in speaker is useless on a noisy train or plane, and download a mix of video and audio-only content so you can listen with the screen off and save battery.

If something goes wrong on the trip

If a download will not play, restart the app and try again, and check that the download actually finished before you left. If the app asks you to reconnect, it is the 30-day renewal check, and there is nothing you can do without internet, so this is why you download the night before. As a backup, keep a few videos in Watch Later and a few e-books or podcasts on the device, so a single failed download does not ruin the journey.

Have an entertainment backup plan

Even a well-prepared traveller should have a backup, because devices fail, batteries die, and downloads sometimes refuse to play at the worst moment. Pack a second form of entertainment that needs no screen and little power: an e-book, a podcast downloaded to your phone, or a music playlist. Keep your phone's charger and a power bank in your hand luggage, not in a checked bag, and keep a paper copy of essential information, such as hotel addresses and booking references, in case the phone dies entirely. If you are travelling with children, pack a small non-digital activity too, because a bored child with a dead tablet is a problem no download can solve. The backup plan is not pessimism; it is the reason a single failure never ruins a journey.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far ahead should I download?

Download the night before on Wi-Fi. Downloads expire after 30 days, so do not download weeks early.

Will downloads work in airplane mode?

Yes. Downloaded videos play with no connection at all, which is exactly what airplane mode needs.

How many videos fit on a phone?

At 480p, roughly 40 hours of video fit in 20 GB. At 1080p, the same space holds about 10 hours.
