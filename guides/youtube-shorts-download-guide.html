YouTube Shorts Download Guide: Save Short Vertical Videos

How to save YouTube Shorts on phone and PC, what the limits are, and how to keep the quality high.

YouTube Shorts are short vertical videos, usually under a minute, designed to be watched on a phone. Saving one for offline viewing works much like saving a regular video, but there are quirks: the links look different, the quality options are limited, and the vertical format behaves differently on a computer. This guide explains how Shorts links work, how to save them with the official app, and what to expect for quality when you watch them later.

How Shorts links work

A Shorts link looks like youtube.com/shorts/VIDEO_ID, where the ID is the same eleven-character code used by regular videos. That means any tool that accepts a YouTube link, including our video-info and thumbnail tools, also accepts a Shorts link. If you copy a link from the Share button in the Shorts player, it may be a short youtu.be link instead, which also works. The video ID is the key part, and it is the same regardless of which link format you copy.

Saving Shorts in the app

In the YouTube app, open the Short, tap the three-dot menu or the Share button, and look for the Download option if you have Premium. The Short is saved to your Downloads list and plays offline. Not every Short offers the button, because the uploader can disable downloads and because music licensing sometimes blocks saving. When the button is missing, that is a deliberate restriction, and no legitimate tool can override it.

Quality and vertical format

Shorts are recorded vertically, usually at 1080 by 1920 pixels, and they are compressed more aggressively than long-form videos because they are meant to load fast on mobile data. A saved Short will look best on a phone held vertically. On a computer the video appears as a narrow column with empty space on either side, which is normal for vertical video. If a Short looks soft, it was probably uploaded at a lower resolution, and downloading it again will not sharpen it.

Sharing Shorts instead of saving

In many cases sharing is better than saving. The Share button gives you a link you can send to a friend, post in a chat, or save in a notes app, and the link always points to the current version of the Short. If you want to keep a collection, create a playlist in the YouTube app and add Shorts to it. That way you keep access without storing files, and the creator keeps the views and engagement that help their channel grow.

Shorts and your data plan

Shorts are small individually, but they are designed to be watched in long sessions, and the data adds up fast. A single Short uses only a few megabytes, but an hour of scrolling through Shorts can use more than a gigabyte, because each video loads automatically and plays on a loop. If you are on a limited mobile plan, that is worth knowing. The YouTube app has a data-saver mode that reduces the quality of Shorts and videos on mobile data, and it is worth enabling if you watch Shorts while commuting. On Wi-Fi, quality does not matter for your bill, but it does matter for your storage if you download Shorts: a saved Short at full quality is larger than it looks. Download only the Shorts you will actually rewatch, and delete them when you are done, because a phone full of Shorts you never open is just wasted space.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I download Shorts with YouTube Premium?

Yes, eligible Shorts can be downloaded in the YouTube app the same way as regular videos.

Why do Shorts look blurry after downloading?

Shorts are vertical and heavily compressed. Saving at the highest available quality and watching on a phone screen gives the best result.

Can I re-upload someone else's Short?

No. Re-uploading another creator's Short without permission infringes copyright and can earn a strike.
