How to Download YouTube Videos on iPhone: The Safe Options

Save YouTube videos on iPhone using the official app and iOS features, without risky third-party apps.

iPhone owners have fewer download options than Android users, because iOS is stricter about what apps can do, but the official route works well and is the one worth using. This guide explains how to save videos with YouTube Premium on iOS, how to use built-in iPhone features such as screen recording for your own content, and how to manage storage so downloads do not crowd out your photos. It also explains why the downloader apps that appear in the App Store are usually a poor choice.

Use YouTube Premium on iOS

Install the YouTube app, sign in, and open a video. Tap Download under the player and choose a quality. The video appears under Library, then Downloads, and plays offline. The same 30-day renewal rule applies: reconnect to the internet at least once a month so the app can verify the download. This is the only method Apple and YouTube both support, and it is the only one that keeps working after app updates. If you already pay for Premium on another device, the same subscription covers your iPhone.

Screen recording for your own videos

iOS has a built-in screen recorder: add it in Settings, then Control Centre, then swipe down and tap the record button. This is useful for recording your own uploaded video, a tutorial you made, or a video call, but it records everything on screen, including notifications, so turn on Do Not Disturb first. Remember that recording someone else's copyrighted video to redistribute it is not made legal by the fact that the tool is built into the phone.

Manage storage on iPhone

Downloaded videos count against your iPhone storage, and iPhones have no SD card slot, so quality choice matters more here than on Android. A 720p download is a good balance for a phone screen. Check usage in Settings, then General, then iPhone Storage, where YouTube will show how much space its downloads use. Delete watched videos promptly, and set downloads to Wi-Fi only in the YouTube app settings so a long video does not consume your mobile data.

Avoid risky downloader apps

Apps that promise to download any YouTube video on iPhone usually work by opening the video in a hidden browser and saving the stream, which breaks YouTube's terms and often breaks when YouTube changes something. Many are removed from the App Store within weeks, and the replacements that appear are frequently ad-heavy or ask for tracking permission. If an app asks you to install a configuration profile or a custom certificate to download videos, decline: that gives the app deep access to your network traffic. Stick to the official app.

Keep iOS and the app updated

Download problems on iPhone are often fixed by something unglamorous: an update. Apple and Google both change how downloads work, and the YouTube app is updated frequently to keep up. An old version of the app can lose the Download button, fail to finish downloads, or refuse to play videos that were saved with a newer version. Check the App Store for YouTube updates before a trip, and install iOS updates when they appear, because they include fixes for storage, networking, and playback. The same applies to your storage: iOS needs free space to work properly, and a phone that is almost full will fail downloads and behave strangely in other ways too. Keep at least a few gigabytes free, delete apps you do not use, and let iCloud handle photo storage. A maintained phone downloads reliably; a neglected one does not.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I download YouTube videos directly to the iPhone Photos app?

Not from the YouTube app. Downloads stay inside YouTube. Screen recording can save a copy to Photos, but only use it for content you own or have permission to record.

Does YouTube Premium work on iPhone?

Yes. Premium works the same on iOS, including offline downloads inside the YouTube app.

Why do iPhone downloader apps keep disappearing?

Apple removes apps that break YouTube's terms, so they are unreliable and often replaced by copycat apps with ads.
