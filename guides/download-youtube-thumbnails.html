How to Download YouTube Thumbnails in Full Quality

Get any YouTube video's thumbnail image in high resolution, including maxresdefault, with no software.

Every YouTube video has a thumbnail image, and YouTube stores it at several sizes on its image servers. If you are a creator who lost your original thumbnail file, or you need to check how a thumbnail looks at full size, you can fetch these images directly with no software at all. This guide explains the thumbnail URL pattern, which sizes exist, how to save the best one, and the copyright rules that apply when you reuse someone else's image.

The thumbnail URL pattern

YouTube thumbnails live at i.ytimg.com with the video ID in the path. The pattern is https://i.ytimg.com/vi/VIDEO_ID/maxresdefault.jpg, where VIDEO_ID is the eleven-character code in the video's URL. Replace maxresdefault with hqdefault, mqdefault, or sddefault for smaller versions. The default.jpg size is 120 by 90, mqdefault is 320 by 180, hqdefault is 480 by 360, sddefault is 640 by 480, and maxresdefault is 1280 by 720. Paste the URL into your browser to view the image, then right-click and save it.

Find the video ID

The video ID is the part after v= in a watch URL, for example the code in youtube.com/watch?v=dQw4w9WgXcQ. Short links like youtu.be/dQw4w9WgXcQ put the ID after the slash, and Shorts URLs put it after /shorts/. Our thumbnail tool on this site does this extraction for you: paste any YouTube link and it shows every available size with a preview, so you do not have to build the URLs by hand.

When a size is missing

Not every video has every size. Videos uploaded before high-resolution thumbnails were common, and videos without a custom thumbnail, may return an error for maxresdefault. When that happens, try hqdefault, which exists for virtually every video. If hqdefault looks soft or pixelated, that is because the source image was small; no tool can add detail that was never uploaded. Creators should always upload a 1280 by 720 custom thumbnail to make sure the highest quality is available.

Copyright and fair use

A thumbnail is a creative work owned by the channel that made it, and copying it for your own video, website, or social post without permission is infringement, even if you change it slightly. The legitimate uses are narrow: checking your own thumbnail, using it in a review or commentary under fair use, or with the creator's permission. If you are a creator, keep your original thumbnail files backed up in a folder with the video project, so you never need to recover them from YouTube's servers in the first place.

Designing a thumbnail that gets clicked

If you are a creator recovering a thumbnail, it is worth understanding what makes one work, so the replacement you upload is better than the one you lost. A good thumbnail has one clear subject, a face showing emotion where that fits the video, and at most three or four words in large, bold text. The background should be simple and high contrast, because the image will be seen at a tiny size on a phone. Avoid clutter: a thumbnail that tries to show everything shows nothing. Use the same colours and style across your videos so viewers recognise your channel at a glance, and test two versions when you are unsure which works better. YouTube's analytics show the click-through rate for each video, which tells you honestly whether a thumbnail is working. Save your original thumbnail files with the video project, at 1280 by 720 pixels, so you never need to recover them from YouTube again.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the highest thumbnail quality?

maxresdefault is 1280 by 720 pixels when the uploader provided a high-resolution thumbnail. Otherwise hqdefault at 480 by 360 is the best available.

Can I reuse someone's thumbnail?

No, not without permission. Thumbnails are copyrighted images owned by the video creator.

Why is maxresdefault sometimes missing?

Older videos or videos uploaded without a custom thumbnail may only have the standard sizes, so the high-resolution version returns a 404 error.
